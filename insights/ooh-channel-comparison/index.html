I’ve built this comparison around the OOH formats I actually want a client to consider when we are deciding where a budget should go. The question is not simply which format is cheapest. It is what the format can do for the campaign.

A billboard gives you a fixed location. A bus campaign gives you route repetition. Rail puts you into commuter journeys. The London Underground adds huge frequency and dwell. Airports create a premium attention environment. Taxi advertising can put a message both outside and inside the vehicle. AdVans and mobile billboards are different again because you can take the media to the audience.

I would not choose a format because it happens to have the lowest rate card price. I would start with the audience, objective and geography, then work backwards to the media.

The important difference: AdVans are not simply a cheaper alternative to a billboard. They are a different planning tool. With a fixed site, the audience comes to the media. Channel profiles

What each channel actually does in a campaign - and when I would reach for it.

Billboards

Billboards are still the straightforward choice for broad awareness. The strength is consistency: the site stays where it is and the audience repeatedly passes it. They are particularly useful when a brand needs a recognisable physical landmark in a town, city or region. See the full billboard advertising guide.

Bus and bus stops

Bus advertising is strongest when repetition and geography matter. Rears give moving visibility, supersides increase physical presence, and shelters give you fixed, eye-level exposure with natural waiting time. A strong bus plan can make a brand feel like it owns a neighbourhood. See the full bus advertising guide.

Rail

Rail is a frequency channel. The strongest planning is about the stations, corridors and audiences that matter rather than simply buying the largest station available. Traincards add longer dwell inside the carriage, while digital formats introduce scheduling flexibility.

London Underground

The Underground is different from roadside OOH because passengers are often stationary or travelling long enough to absorb more information. It is particularly strong for London launches, commuter campaigns, brand credibility and repeated exposure. Lower-entry Tube car panels and outer-zone posters make it possible to build targeted campaigns without automatically going straight to flagship takeovers. See the London Underground advertising guide.

Airport

Airport advertising trades on dwell and context. The audience is concentrated in a premium environment and can spend significant time around the advertising. It makes most sense when the target audience travels or when the campaign benefits from a more premium setting. See the full airport advertising guide.

Taxi

Taxi advertising gives you two environments in one campaign: the exterior audience moving through the city and the passenger inside the vehicle. Screens and interiors are particularly useful when a campaign has a response mechanic or more detailed message. See the full taxi advertising guide.

AdVans / mobile billboards

AdVans are the most route-led option in this comparison. The Standard and Electric formats are 20sqm, while the XL is 35sqm. I would use them when there is a clear reason for the vehicle to be somewhere: agency offices, business districts, retail areas, events, transport hubs, competitor locations or a specific postcode. See the full AdVan & mobile billboard guide.

The big difference is control over geography and timing. A fixed billboard is tied to its site. An AdVan can change its route around the audience. That makes it particularly interesting for B2B, challenger brands, launches, retail and events.

Budget thinking

The right answer changes as the budget increases. I would rather create real presence in one or two environments than spread a modest budget across seven channels and make every channel look small.

Indicative budgetWhat I would considerReason
Under £1,000 Billboard, bus rear, rail traincard, Tube entry formats or one-day AdVan Several channels have genuine low-entry options
£1,500–£3,000 Focused AdVan burst, bus/shelter combination, selected Tube activity or local billboard plan Enough budget to create concentrated presence
£3,000–£10,000 Two-channel regional plan or sustained AdVan / bus / rail activity Frequency and depth become more important
£10,000–£25,000 London Underground, coordinated AdVan activity, rail + roadside or bus + taxi The campaign can build genuine scale in a defined geography
£25,000+ Multi-channel London / regional campaign Use channels for different jobs rather than simply adding formats
A useful way to think about the mix: Reach + frequency + relevance. Billboards and bus can provide reach. Rail and the Underground can add frequency. Taxi can add an interior response environment. AdVans can add relevance by physically taking the campaign into the places where the audience is concentrated. Airports can add premium attention.

What I would avoid

  • Choosing the format before defining the audience.
  • Comparing only the cheapest unit price.
  • Buying the biggest site, station or airport simply because it looks impressive on a media plan.
  • Spreading a modest budget so thinly that no channel gets enough repetition.
  • Treating an AdVan as a gimmick rather than planning the route as part of the media.
  • Assuming one AdVan gives blanket city coverage.
  • Using the same creative approach for a Tube panel, roadside billboard and moving vehicle.
  • Ignoring production, posting, installation, operating requirements and lead times when calculating the real campaign cost.

The final answer

If you want broad, fixed visibility, start with billboards. If you want to own a neighbourhood, look at bus and bus stops. If commuters are the audience, rail is a natural fit. If the audience is in London and frequency matters, the Underground becomes very compelling. If the environment and dwell are part of the brand proposition, look at airports. If you want exterior visibility plus a captive passenger audience, taxi is worth serious consideration.

If you have a very specific audience, location, event, business district or timing requirement, I would put AdVans firmly on the shortlist. The advantage is not simply that the billboard moves. It is that the media plan can move with the audience.

That is the point of this comparison. There is no universal best OOH format. There is a best format for the job the campaign actually needs to do.
