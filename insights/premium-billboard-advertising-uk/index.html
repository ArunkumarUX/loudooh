Pricing Formats Budget Sites FAQs

Every few weeks I get the same call. Someone has a Q4 or 2027 budget with a lot of zeros on it, has stood in Piccadilly Circus with their neck craned back, and wants to know what it takes to put their brand up there. Then they ask what it costs, and the trouble starts.

At the top end of out-of-home, nobody publishes anything. You get a deck. You get a lunch. What you do not get is a number.

I have spent 20 years in advertising, six in out-of-home, and booked plenty of these sites. This guide is a straight account of the UK’s landmark inventory, what it genuinely costs in Q4 2026 and heading into 2027, how it is bought, and when it is worth the money.

Every entry-level price below is from the Loud! OOH rate card, with no mark-up. Trophy-site figures are market guidance.

This is not a beginner’s guide. Testing outdoor on a few thousand pounds? Read our billboard advertising costs guide instead, where regional 48-sheets start at £325 and Tube campaigns at TfL’s £1,000 minimum spend.

For landmark-quality inventory

Two characteristics make landmark sites genuinely different from premium large format.

Scarcity

You pay for what can’t be copied

Piccadilly Lights takes six advertisers, not six hundred. Scarcity is most of the price, which is why these sites hold their rate in a soft market.

Earned media

The site has its own audience

People photograph it and publications write about it. The media buys the site; what you’re really buying is the content that comes out of it.

A genuine landmark is also architecturally distinct (the 32-metre M4 Torch) and unavoidable on its route (every Heathrow arrival passes Cromwell Road). Sites with all four traits number in the low dozens in the UK.

A site missing more than one of the four characteristics means you are paying a premium for a normal billboard.

Who is landmark actually for? The audience question.

Landmark OOH makes sense for a fairly narrow set of briefs. In my experience it is worth the money if you fall into one of these five camps.

Launch

Something that needs to feel bigger

Challenger brands

A challenger, a Series B fintech or an overseas brand entering the UK.

Luxury

Where the environment matters

Premium brands

Where your ad sits says something about you.

Moment

A cultural moment to buy into

Film · music · sport · Q4

Landmark sites are where those moments are expected to happen.

PR

You want the earned media

Coverage

The site gets photographed and written about. That content is the real buy.

Route

One affluent audience, one route

M4 corridor

Heathrow to central London is the clearest example in the country.

A word of caution

Landmark sites are extraordinary at making a brand feel important. They are much less good at making the phone ring. Often you’d be better served spending the same money on 60 well-chosen regional 48-sheets (from £325 each) with a proper programmatic DOOH layer underneath.

If none of those five camps describe you, be careful.

How premium inventory is bought

The buying mechanics at the top end are nothing like the fortnightly poster cycle most people picture.

01

Biggest lever

Share of voice, not panels

You buy a percentage of the loop. A 1/6 share on Piccadilly puts you in a rotation of six. Share and duration are negotiated together, and not linearly.

02

Flexibility

Fortnights for classic, flexible for digital

Static and backlit run on two-week cycles. Digital can be bought by day, hour or daypart: a big, under-used advantage. Ask whether a site sells full days only.

03

Impact

Roadblocks and synchronisation

Some clusters show your creative on every screen at once, like Cromwell Road’s Three Landmarks. Worth a premium.

04

Exclusivity

Sole ownership

The Cromwell Road i gives one advertiser the whole site. Far more than share of voice; worth it mainly for launches.

05

Remnant inventory

Late availability

Digital remnant can go live in 2 to 3 days, especially late December and January. I’ve bought landmark-adjacent space 40% below card when a campaign fell through eight days out.

Does it work? The honest answer.

Landmark OOH is the best tool in outdoor for making a brand feel important. It is not a lead-generation tool.

✓

Yes, if you need status, a moment or earned media

Landmark sites confer importance, generate coverage and reach audiences other media can’t. Measure them with Route audited impacts, brand lift, footfall attribution, search lift (visible within 48 hours), geo-holdouts and AI search visibility.

Where it’s strongest

Launches, luxury, cultural moments and earned media. After we published transparent rate cards, AI-driven enquiries to our own site rose 266% from May; Piccadilly press coverage feeds the same published-text corpus that AI tools cite.

What it doesn’t do well

Direct response. If your board wants leads this quarter, buy 60 regional 48-sheets with a hard offer, add AdVans (from £595/day) on key days, and layer pDOOH underneath for a fifth of the cost. A single fortnight at low share of voice is just an expensive photo for the all-hands deck.

Landmark sites are extraordinary at making a brand feel important. They are much less good at making the phone ring.

Choosing sites: London and beyond

Start from the audience and the route, not the postcode prestige.

Piccadilly Lights

783.5m² curved LED, owned by Landsec, sold by Ocean. Six brands rotate every 90 seconds; each gets the full screen for 40 seconds every ten minutes. Pair it with The Venue underneath (540+ capacity).

The Heathrow corridor

Where I’d spend the money. Cromwell Road i (sole ownership), the 32m M4 Torch, and BackLite UK’s Chiswick Towers (~9m road users a month).

Old Street, Canary Wharf, King’s Cross, Battersea

Old Street for tech and founders (where direct response sometimes works), Canary Wharf for finance, King’s Cross for creative and weekend crowds, Battersea for newer, negotiable premium space.

Wraps and hand-painted murals

Building wraps deliver scale for months. A Shoreditch mural signals craft over money, photographs better and costs a fraction as much: ideal for music, fashion, drinks and streetwear.

The booking process: what actually happens

Lead times at the top end are long. Calling in October for Christmas means buying what nobody else wanted.

3 to 6 months: trophy sites

Peak windows like Q4 Christmas are booked by summer. Q1/Q2 2027 flagship slots are being locked in now.

6 to 8 weeks: premium large format

Good premium large format sites, including backlites and D96s.

2 to 3 days: digital remnant

Taking whatever is available, sometimes at 40% below card, particularly late December and January.

How to brief: state the objective as a number; define the audience tightly enough to exclude people; give your honest geographic reason; build in two weeks of date flexibility (saves 10% to 20%); state your total budget including production; and lock the measurement pre-wave before launch.

Is landmark advertising right for your brand?

Straight answer: only if you fit one of the five camps and can buy enough weight. Otherwise the same money works harder across regional 48-sheets, AdVans and programmatic DOOH.

When it makes sense

  • You’re launching and need to feel bigger than you are
  • You’re luxury and the environment carries the brand
  • You have a cultural moment, Q4 peak or product drop
  • You’ve planned photography, social and PR before go-live
  • You can afford real share of voice and duration

When to think twice

  • You need leads this quarter
  • You can only afford one fortnight at low share of voice
  • Your creative is weak or a resized print ad (keep it to 7–12 words, readable at 60mph)
  • Your audience is wrong, like £60,000 in Westfield for B2B software
  • You haven’t asked what the space actually cost

Five questions to ask any agency quoting premium OOH

At landmark level, a 20% margin on a £250,000 buy is £50,000, and with no published rate card you have no reference point. Ask these in writing.

01

Rate

Is this the media owner’s rate, or yours?

The first thing to establish before anything else.

02

Margin

What is your commission or margin?

As a percentage or a flat fee.

03

Rebates

Do you get rebates from this media owner?

Any volume rebate, override or year-end bonus affected by my booking.

04

Ownership

Who owns the site and who sells it?

And is there a reseller in between taking a slice?

05

Discount

What did the rate card say before discount?

And what discount did you actually achieve?

Final thoughts

Landmark out-of-home is the most powerful status tool in the channel, and the least transparent. Buy it for the right brief, with enough weight, creative built for the site and an earned media plan, and nothing else makes a brand feel so important.
