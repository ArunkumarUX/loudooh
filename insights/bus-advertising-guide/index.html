First, the numbers that actually matter

Buses carried around 3.4 billion passenger journeys in England alone in the year to March 2025, according to Department for Transport data, with London accounting for roughly half of that. Bus remains the most used form of public transport in the UK by a wide margin - more journeys than the entire national rail network and the London Underground combined.

For advertisers, passenger numbers are only half the story. A bus advert is seen mostly by people who are not on the bus.

Street-level

Goes where billboards cannot

There are streets in every UK town where no billboard exists and none ever will - and a bus route runs down them every ten minutes. Where the route pauses, the shelter takes over: more than 20,000 advertising shelters sit at eye level on the pavement.

Fixed network

Repetition built in

A bus runs the same roads all day, every day. People who live, work, or shop along those roads see the same campaign again and again. Add the shelters and you get the fixed points too.

A well-planned national bus campaign can reach up to 71% of the UK population inside two weeks, with cost per thousand from as little as £3.

Who actually sees bus advertising? The audience question.

Bus advertising has three audiences, and they are very different sizes. Understanding which formats reach which is fundamental to whether a campaign works.

Whole route

Exterior audience

Mass local · every day

Everyone the bus passes: pedestrians, drivers at lights, people at stops, shoppers, school runs, office workers. It targets a geography, not a demographic. Most UK bus advertising is booked by depot rather than individual route - still genuinely local, but ask for it in writing if someone promises one specific route.

2–4 min

Waiting audience

Shelter dwell · pavement

People standing at the stop, plus everyone walking or driving past it all day. Two to four minutes of natural dwell, and the panel never moves. Habitual users mean billboard-grade frequency - bookable in targeted postcode clusters, the sharpest local targeting in outdoor.

~20 min

Interior audience

Passengers · long dwell

Around 3.4 billion journeys a year in England. Passengers skew younger adults, students, and older residents - locally rooted. Interior media is priced by route and line, and bundles cheaply with exterior buys.

The practical rule

Exteriors are for reach and brand presence across a geography. Shelters are for holding the fixed points where your audience stands still. Interiors are for messages that need reading time. The best bus campaigns usually combine them, because each additional audience comes cheap once you are paying for the first.

Bus advertising is a mass local medium. It does not target a demographic; it targets a geography.

What a real campaign looks like at different budget levels

Bus advertising is a fleet medium: single-bus campaigns exist but the format works through repetition across a network. Here is what different budgets genuinely buy.

Under £1,500

Local

Genuine local presence

Ten to fifteen bus rears across your local depot’s routes can cost from under £1,000 for two weeks. Or take two or three shelter panels at the stops nearest your premises from £300 each and own the pavement your customers actually stand on. For a local business, either is a serious campaign.

£1.5k – £5k

Town

Town coverage, both audiences

Ten to fifteen supersides across the depot fleet, plus a cluster of shelter panels on your busiest corridors. Add interior panels through a bundled buy and the same budget works the pavement, the traffic and the passengers simultaneously.

£5k – £15k

City

City coverage or multi-town

Twenty to forty buses across a city’s main corridors, or a mixed-format campaign combining T-sides for impact with rears for drivers and digital shelter screens in the centre. Local people start to feel the brand is everywhere.

£15k+

Landmark

Wraps, dominance and landmark campaigns

Larger fleets across a major city, coordinated multi-town coverage, or full wraps. A wrapped double decker moving through a city centre is a campaign people photograph - and at this level bus reliably earns social and press coverage on top of paid exposure.

The factors that move the price

Five variables explain most of the difference between two otherwise similar campaigns.

01

Biggest lever

London versus regional

London prices well above the regional equivalent, reflecting TfL network scale, audience density, and demand. Regional and town fleets are where the value sits for most SMEs.

02

Volume

Fleet size and volume discounts

Per-bus rates fall as fleet size rises. Supersides carry a practical minimum of around 10 buses, with discounts stepping in from 20 buses upwards. Committing to volume upfront always beats building incrementally at open rates.

03

Package

Format mix

Rears, sides, shelters and interiors are priced separately but bundle well. Running multiple formats through one booking is consistently cheaper - and means the campaign works the pavement, the traffic, the queue, and the passengers simultaneously.

04

Timing

Campaign duration and contract structure

Most exterior and shelter formats trade in two-week periods, with rates improving on longer bookings. Mega rears and regional wraps are typically annual contracts - headline prices look large but effective monthly rates are competitive.

05

Total cost

Production

Printed vinyl for rears and sides typically adds £40 to £90 per panel including fitting. Shelter posters print cheaply. Full wraps require specialist installation, quoted separately. Digital formats carry no production cost.

Production rule: Always build print and fitting into the total from the start - especially on wrap campaigns where specialist installation is a major line.

Does it work? The honest answer on effectiveness.

Bus advertising works through repetition and proximity. Its effectiveness case rests on four things that are genuinely true - and one limitation that matters.

✓

Yes - the best value geographic saturation tool in UK outdoor

And the bus rear is the cheapest genuine attention you can buy anywhere in the medium. It rewards brands with a defined local or city catchment, simple bold creative, and the patience to let repetition compound.

Street-level presence where nothing else reaches

Bus is the only large-format channel present on most UK high streets and residential corridors. Local recall studies consistently show strong prompted awareness among residents of the areas covered, precisely because the exposure repeats daily.

The captive driver

Bus rears average 48 seconds of dwell from following traffic in congestion. From £60 per panel, it is pound for pound the cheapest attention in UK outdoor - short-copy territory for phone numbers, web addresses, and single-line offers.

The queue that reads

A shelter panel gets two to four minutes of standing dwell from the same local people, day after day - billboard-grade frequency with genuine reading time, plus postcode-level booking precision.

Interior dwell, bundled cheaply

Twenty minutes of passenger dwell is the quiet bargain of UK outdoor when bundled into an exterior buy. Recruitment, colleges, local health services, and QR-code-led campaigns perform well here.

Where it's strongest

Geographic saturation at street level, captive rear exposure, shelter dwell with postcode precision, and long-dwell interiors at low package prices - especially for brands with a defined local catchment and bold, brief creative.

What bus advertising does not do well

Exterior exposure is brief and moving - detail belongs on shelters and interiors. Booking is depot-based rather than route-precise. The audience is broad rather than targeted, so premium B2B brands will find less concentration than on the Underground or at airports. Impression figures are modelled estimates - treat them as directional. For verified measurement, Route data is the industry standard.

It rewards brands with a defined local or city catchment, simple bold creative, and the patience to let repetition compound. It punishes complicated messages and scattergun planning.

Choosing your coverage: a practical framework

Match the coverage to your catchment - not just the biggest fleet number.

Local businesses: buy your own patch

Identify the depot network covering your catchment and concentrate there, then buy the shelter panels at the stops nearest your door. A gym, dental practice, or restaurant advertising on the buses its customers see and the stops they wait at is buying the most relevant impressions available at any price.

City campaigns: cover the corridors

Every city has main bus corridors where dozens of routes converge on the centre. Fleet coverage weighted to those depots puts your campaign on the busiest streets all day, and the shelters along the same corridors carry the highest footfall in the network.

Regional campaigns: town by town

Outside the big cities, bus advertising is often the only OOH channel with real presence in market towns and suburbs. Regional brands can build coverage town by town at regional rates, reaching communities national media plans routinely miss.

London: zones and boroughs

Campaigns are planned by area - boroughs, corridors, or the West End and City. One of the more affordable ways to buy visible, moving presence in the capital, sitting well below Underground entry costs while covering ground the Tube does not.

The booking process: what actually happens

Allow three to four weeks from brief to buses on the road for standard printed formats. Artwork approval takes a few days, print and fitting one to two weeks, and buses are fitted as they cycle through the depot. Full wraps need four to six weeks minimum. Shelter campaigns are quicker: two weeks from artwork approval to panels live is typical, and digital shelters can go live in days.

Week 1 - Brief & coverage plan

Depot selection, shelter clusters, format mix, fleet size, and rate confirmation.

Week 2 - Artwork

Creative sign-off, CAP compliance, and vehicle / shelter template specs.

Weeks 3–4 - Production & live

Print and depot fitting for vehicles; shelters can be live sooner, digital in days.

UK bus and shelter inventory is controlled by media owners holding contracts with bus operators and local authorities. A multi-city campaign usually means multiple owners - booking through an agency consolidates the plan, paperwork, and pricing leverage.

Compliance: All creative must comply with the CAP Code. Nothing that could be mistaken for road signage, restrictions on certain categories, and operator-level approval on all artwork. Rear designs must work around number plates and light clusters - build to the actual template. Submit artwork at least five working days before fitting.

Is bus advertising right for an SME?

Straight answer: Bus and bus stop advertising together are arguably the most SME-friendly channel in UK outdoor. Bus rears start from £60 per panel, shelters from £300, the targeting mechanism is geography rather than expensive data, and the format reaches exactly the local audiences most small businesses live on.

When it makes sense

  • Your customers are concentrated in a specific town, city, or set of neighbourhoods
  • You want repeated local exposure rather than one-off reach
  • Your brand benefits from feeling established and visible in its own community
  • You have a simple, bold message or a strong visual identity
  • You can run long enough for the repetition to compound

When to think twice

  • Your audience is a narrow professional or premium segment better concentrated elsewhere
  • Your message needs study time and you are only buying vehicle exteriors
  • You need precisely measured, trackable response as the primary metric
  • Your catchment does not align with any meaningful depot or corridor pattern
  • Your creative budget cannot stretch to work that reads at a glance from a moving vehicle

How to get more from your bus advertising budget

Before spending on wider fleet coverage, secure the two or three stops nearest your premises.

Six planning moves that consistently improve what a bus budget delivers.

01

Entry

Start with rears if budget is tight

From £60 per panel, bus rears are the cheapest genuine attention in UK outdoor, aimed at the one audience with nowhere else to look. For a first OOH buy, a set of rears across your local depot beats almost anything else at the price.

02

Shelters

Buy the stops outside your own door

The most precise purchase in this guide is the shelter panel your actual customers stand at. It is the bus advertising equivalent of owning your own shop window, twice.

03

Creative

Design for three seconds and thirty metres

Exterior bus creative is read at a glance. One visual, one message, legible branding. If the design needs explaining, it needs redesigning. Save the detail for the shelters and interiors, where the audience has time.

04

Response

Put the call to action on the rear

Drivers behind the bus have time to read and remember. Sides build the brand; the rear closes. Phone number, web address, or offer on the rear, brand story on the sides.

05

Interior

Bundle the interiors

Interior media prices by route and bundles cheaply with exterior buys, delivering twenty minutes of dwell. For any campaign with detail to communicate, the interior bundle is the cheapest incremental audience in the plan.

06

Measurement

Run search alongside, as always

Bus campaigns lift local branded search. Paid search on brand and category terms during the campaign captures the interest the buses create, and Google Search Console gives the cleanest free read on local registration.

Final thoughts

Bus advertising is the street-level workhorse of UK outdoor. The vehicles go where no other large format exists and repeat across their network every day. The shelters hold the fixed points where the same local audience stands still, with the sharpest postcode targeting in the medium. Together they are one medium bought in two halves, and pound for pound they saturate a local geography better than anything else you can buy - with entry points, from a £60 bus rear to a £300 shelter, that no other large-format channel can touch.

The campaigns that win choose their patch like premises, buy the stops outside their own door, keep the creative bold and brief, put the call to action on the rear, bundle the interiors, and run long enough for the repetition to do its work.
The campaigns that disappoint buy scattered coverage with complicated artwork for two weeks and conclude the medium does not work. It does. It just rewards discipline.
