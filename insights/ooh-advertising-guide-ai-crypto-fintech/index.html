Three of my last five enquiries came from tech founders: an AI agent platform, a registered crypto exchange and a payments business fresh from a Series A. All three asked a version of the same question.

Does out of home actually work for a company like ours, or is it just an expensive way to make the board feel good?

The biggest lever on a tight budget

Two characteristics make OOH genuinely different from the auctions tech brands live in.

Fixed price

Not an auction

Paid social and search get pricier as categories crowd. OOH is a fixed space at a fixed rate that doesn’t inflate because a rival raised a round last Tuesday.

Costly signal

Trust you can’t fake

A 48-sheet tells people you are real: everyone understands a fly-by-night operation does not buy a wall. That perception is the product; the impressions are a bonus.

Reach at scale is cheap too: a national bus rear buy reaches up to 71% of the UK in two weeks from £3 per thousand, and the London Underground carries 1.216bn journeys a year across 272 stations.

You cannot buy trust programmatically, but you can buy it geographically.

The compliance rules that move your timeline

Not legal advice: get your approver to sign everything off. But these are where tech campaigns fall over in the final week.

01

Crypto

Four lawful routes only

Since 8 October 2023, crypto promotions sit under section 21 FSMA. Only an FCA-authorised firm, an FCA-registered cryptoasset firm, an exempt person or a section 21 approver can communicate or approve one. AML registration gives no approval rights.

02

Crypto

The prescribed risk warning

It must begin “Don’t invest unless you’re prepared to lose all the money you invest.” Prominent, not four-point type in a corner.

03

Crypto

Cooling-off and banned incentives

A 24-hour cooling-off period applies to first-time investors on direct offer pages. Incentives to invest, including refer-a-friend bonuses, are banned.

04

TfL

Crypto copy referral

TfL reviews all crypto advertising before it runs. Add two to three weeks purely for clearance.

05

AI

CAP Code substantiation

Every claim needs evidence. “The most accurate model in the world” or “First AI agent for X” will be sent back without it.

Does it work for tech? The honest answer.

OOH works for tech when you measure it like a brand channel, not like paid search.

✓

Yes, if you buy the right places and measure it properly

OOH fixes rising acquisition costs and flat branded search. Paid media captures existing demand; OOH creates it. When we rewrote our own pages with exact figures, enquiries from AI search rose 266% between May and July, because AI tools cite specific, published numbers.

How to measure it

Set a 4-week baseline (branded search, direct traffic, organic sessions, demos, sign-ups). Add a free-text “How did you hear about us?”: people type “saw your poster at Moorgate”. Run a geo holdout (run Manchester, hold Leeds) and judge B2B on 60-day pipeline in target postcodes.

What it doesn’t do well

Last-click attribution. Don’t judge a two-week buy at two weeks: recall is still 79% at 30 days, with £1.60 to £1.80 returned per £1. And programmatic loops can’t replace the permanence of a 48-sheet when credibility is the point.

A screen in a rotating loop is an impression. A 48-sheet with your name on it for a fortnight is a statement.

Choosing your mix: a practical framework

Start from your buyer’s week, not a national plan.

AI company

Escalator panel runs to explain in sequence (from £100), Tube car panels for the long version (from £10), one 48-sheet where your buyers are, and an AdVan at your flagship conference (from £595/day).

Crypto business

Large format static at high-credibility locations. Build the risk warning in at design stage. Expect extra lead time for Tube and rail copy referral. Avoid clever.

Fintech or payments

Zone 1 Underground (Bank, Moorgate, Liverpool Street, Canary Wharf) for enterprise trust, postcode shelters (from £300) for consumers, rail corridors for frequency. Time it around your funding announcement.

B2B SaaS

Six to ten sites in one business district for four weeks, airport digital where buyers travel (from £295/wk), and taxi tip seats (£40–£85+) and cab screens (£25–£35+) in the target postcode.

The six-week plan: what actually happens

Digital can go live in 2 to 3 days; print needs 2 to 4 weeks, plus 2 to 3 weeks for FCA/TfL clearance.

Week 1: Audience and baseline

Define the audience by location (shortlist 6 to 10 sites) and set your 4-week measurement baseline.

Week 2: Mix and approvals

Lock the format mix and budget; start FCA and TfL approval.

Week 3: Creative

7 to 12 words maximum on large format; long copy only on interiors.

Week 4: Sign-off and production

Compliance sign-off, copy referral and print production.

Week 5: Go live

Go live and photograph every site.

Week 6+: Measure

Measure at day 30 and day 60 against your baseline and holdout.

Q4 timing: central London Q4 space is tight, but late-December and January remnant inventory regularly opens up at reduced rates if you are flexible.

Is OOH right for your tech brand?

Straight answer: yes, if your CAC is rising, branded search is flat or you need to look bigger before a raise. Start regional, start specific, and measure it properly.

When it makes sense

  • Your paid acquisition costs keep climbing
  • Your category has a trust problem to overcome
  • Your buyers cluster in a few predictable places
  • You’re timing a funding or product announcement
  • You’ll set a baseline and measure over 60 days

Nine mistakes tech brands make

  • Buying London for vanity over regional at 40–45% less
  • A full homepage on a 48-sheet
  • Ignoring dwell time
  • Judging a 2-week buy inside 14 days
  • Leaving FCA/TfL compliance to week 5
  • QR codes on roadside, escalators or restricted Tube formats
  • No pre-campaign baseline
  • pDOOH loops when you need static permanence
  • Paying undisclosed agency markups

How to write creative that works on a wall

The most common failure: a founder trying to fit their homepage onto a poster.

01

Word count

Seven to twelve words, maximum

Drivers get two to three seconds. Seven words on fast roads; never more than twelve on large format.

02

Focus

One idea and a legible brand name

Not three benefits. If nobody can read your name across a dual carriageway or platform, nothing else matters.

03

Dwell

Match copy and QR codes to dwell

Long copy and QR codes go on stationary, high-dwell formats. Use a short, memorable URL everywhere else.

04

Conditions

Contrast over cleverness

Your poster will be seen in Q4 rain, at dusk and behind buses. Make it legible first.

05

Compliance

Design the risk warning in

If you’re regulated, put the FCA warning into layout one, not the approval round.
